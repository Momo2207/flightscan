Airspace / field notes

A small window into your sky.

Appearance

Midnight pairs ink and icy blue light. Sunset uses warm cream, sand and apricot. Appearance controls glass effects and optional flowing gradients. Wall displays retain an independent saved theme. Reduced effects use opaque surfaces; reduced-motion preferences stop decorative animation. The embedded Roboto sans-serif font includes genuine Thin, Light, Regular, Medium, Bold and Black weights. Font license: Apache 2.0.

Display notes

Reported observations; wall mode interpolates delayed positions. Not for navigation. Routine playback, provider and inference explanations are kept here so the tracker can show the aircraft clearly. Essential feed failures and last-known positions remain labelled.

Read the traffic

Search for a town, enter decimal coordinates, use your device location, or select “Set center” and click the map. Apply a radius from 1–450 km (up to 242.98 NM). Click an aircraft or its callsign to inspect it. Distance and bearing are measured from your chosen center.

Positions come from adsb.fi through your configured relay, with ADSB.lol and OpenSky as automatic fallbacks through the same relay. Connection diagnostics lists the active provider. A callsign is the transmitted flight identifier, which may differ from the booking flight number. Registration and aircraft type are shown when supplied. Airline or operator names are inferred from a recognised three-letter ICAO callsign prefix. This does not confirm the operating carrier, ownership or a booking flight number. Registration-like or unrecognised callsigns show “Operator unknown”. Routes are looked up independently through the relay when enabled.

Altitude is barometric feet, not height above terrain. Groundspeed is knots. Vertical rate is feet per minute. Tracks are observed positions collected while this page is open, not full flight history. Dashed tracks have observation gaps over 45 seconds.

Wall display

Wall display provides Midnight and Sunset layouts for a room screen. Aircraft playback is delayed by 30–120 seconds (90 by default). Intermediate positions and numeric metrics are estimated between known reports, never predicted beyond them. The same timeline drives markers, cards, trails and the Follow camera. Wall noses and direction arrows follow the displayed movement path between reports. Aircraft-only cards keep callsigns fixed and cycle available altitude, groundspeed, aircraft type, route codes and full locations every eight seconds. Missing pages are skipped. Detail text crossfades over 500 ms unless motion is reduced; card dimensions stay fixed through the cycle and arrows stay clear of all cards. Stopped positions use the reported heading or retain their last usable orientation; reception holds freeze the nose with the position. This depicts ground movement, not aircraft yaw or wind-corrected air heading. A first start needs at least two reports; longer delays need more history. Buffer underruns hold the last position for at most 45 seconds of playback time, fading over the final 15 seconds. Gaps over two minutes or implausible position jumps are not joined. Silhouettes show a generic aircraft category, not an exact model drawing. Optional Flight spectrum colours use delayed altitude and vertical rate: blue on reported ground, blue/cyan/green on climb, green at level, then yellow/orange/red on descent and a smooth return to blue on ground. Airborne altitude uses a fixed 0–40,000 ft scale; it is not height above local terrain or confirmation of a flight phase. State changes blend over about eight seconds. Held aircraft retain their last colour; unavailable telemetry fades to the classic colour. Wake lock depends on the browser and device. Fullscreen needs a user action. Use the public Vercel production domain if a generated deployment URL redirects to login.

Three ways to watch

Area keeps your chosen center and radius while you pan. “Search this area” moves that center to the map center. Follow aircraft queries a single ICAO identifier beyond the area radius. Start from an aircraft’s inspector or enter its six-character hex. Panning pauses automatic centring; “Resume centring” returns to the target. Missing reports leave an amber last-known marker and timestamp, never a predicted position. Trails contain only observations collected during this session, up to ten minutes.

Explore fleet combines airline and aircraft type independently, with “All” available for either. Exact type matches its reported ICAO code; type families combine the listed related codes. Airline matching remains inferred from callsigns. Aircraft without a reported type do not match a specific type filter. Coverage is your current area or the last searched map view. Map searches fit within 450 km of their center; zoom in if the view is too wide. Map movement alone never starts a new geographic request. The searched boundary stays visible. These are regional matches, not a worldwide fleet inventory.

Each mode remembers its filters. Save up to 20 named views, including their area or searched bounds, to revisit them. Saving the same name updates it. Saved aircraft and saved views stay in this browser. Share view includes the selected mode, area and airline/type filters, or the followed identifier.

Your aircraft collection

Search by airline name, callsign, registration or aircraft type. Use the operator filter to narrow the list. Save aircraft with the star in the inspector and select “Saved aircraft” to see your favourites currently reporting inside your radius. These are aircraft bookmarks, not saved flights or a flight history. On a phone, tap an aircraft to open its details, then close the sheet or center it on the map.

Aircraft names describe the reported type or type family, not an exact variant or seating configuration. A helicopter marker is used only when the supplied type is recognised as a helicopter. Aircraft symbols distinguish wide-body, narrow-body, regional, business and military jets, propeller aircraft, helicopters and gliders using the reported type. These are design categories, not verified ownership. Missing headings appear as dots. “Level”, “Climbing” and “Descending” use reported vertical rate with a 150 ft/min threshold; they are not flight-plan phases.

Reference data

Airline names and aircraft types use a bundled Virtual Radar Server reference dataset (CC0; snapshot 28 September 2026). Selected brand icons are bundled from Simple Icons (CC0). Other operators use their ICAO code as a badge. Names and logos are for identification, with no affiliation implied. Reference records may be incomplete or outdated. The interface makes no additional network requests for these lookups or logos.

Freshness and coverage

adsb.fi and ADSB.lol refresh every 30 seconds while this tab is visible and a relay is configured. OpenSky is the last fallback and refreshes every 60 seconds. Each provider has its own retry delay. Rate limits are respected across reloads and area changes; the app waits until a provider is eligible before trying it again. OpenSky uses the API credentials configured on Vercel, or anonymous access with a limited daily allowance per IP; it does not supply registration or aircraft type. Older positions are faded after 30 seconds and removed from current results after 90 seconds. Follow mode retains a separately labelled last-known position until another aircraft is followed. Empty results mean no recent positions were reported, not that the sky is empty. Pausing or switching tabs stops polling. Errors trigger increasing retry delays; the app never substitutes sample aircraft.

Publish with Vercel

  1. Upload the supplied project files to GitHub, then import that repository into Vercel. Follow SETUP.md in the download.
  2. Add your OpenSky API client credentials to the Vercel project’s environment variables and deploy.
  3. Open the production Vercel address to use the app and relay together.
  4. To keep GitHub Pages, replace its index.html with this file, then open Connection and enter the Vercel address.

The interface remains a single HTML file with no build step or library CDN. A small relay makes the flight requests on the server and returns readable responses to this page. No flight API keys are stored in the HTML. Internet is needed for flight data, place searches and map tiles. Provider outages, quotas and access changes can still interrupt the feed; the relay does not override those limits.

Airport infrastructure

Gallery maps show mapped runway and taxiway geometry from the existing OpenFreeMap/OpenMapTiles aeroway layer. Runways become visible at tile zoom 10, taxiways at 12. Centre-lines use a screen-width stroke; supplied polygons retain their mapped outline. The map does not infer pavement widths, surveyed edges, threshold markings or runway operating status. Geometry may be simplified or incomplete and is not an operational airport chart. The airport overlay omits airport boundaries, apron/gate shapes and terminals and creates no additional requests.

Wall journey figures

Route distance is the great-circle distance between the two reported airports, not flown or remaining distance. Estimated flight time uses nominal cruise assumptions of 490 kt for wide-body jets, 450 kt for narrow-body/business/generic jets, 440 kt for regional jets and 460 kt for B738, plus a fixed 20-minute climb/descent allowance. Times round to five minutes. This is a whole-flight estimate, not a schedule or ETA; wind, routing, taxi and holding are excluded. Current groundspeed is not used. Missing airport coordinates hide the figures; other aircraft categories omit time. The existing cached route supplies all inputs without extra requests.

Flight routes

Routes are likely callsign matches from ADSB.lol, checked against the reported location. They may be missing or incorrect; destinations, arrivals and progress are not confirmed. Route requests send the callsign and observed aircraft coordinates through your relay to ADSB.lol. They are independent of position polling. Available routes are saved with local sightings; no historic route is fetched after an aircraft leaves.

Service use & privacy
For personal, non-commercial use. adsb.fi’s public API and Open-Meteo’s free geocoding API are non-commercial. ADSB.lol uses dynamic rate limits and may change access requirements. OpenSky anonymous access is limited per IP. Flight requests go through your relay, which allows the configured website origin; provider limits still apply. Successful responses may be cached for 25 seconds for adsb.fi and ADSB.lol or 55 seconds for OpenSky, with original observation timestamps preserved. OpenStreetMap tiles are best effort: normal interactive viewing only, with browser caching and attribution. Larger deployments should arrange appropriate provider capacity.
Your center is sent through your relay to the active flight provider (adsb.fi, ADSB.lol or OpenSky); a typed city query is sent to Open-Meteo. Map tile requests reveal the viewed area to OpenStreetMap. The last area, mode settings, saved views, any saved relay URL, saved aircraft identifiers and provider retry delays are stored only in this browser. Share area includes coordinates and radius in the URL. No analytics are installed.
