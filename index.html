Airspace / field notes

A small window into your sky.

Read the traffic

Search for a town, enter decimal coordinates, use your device location, or select “Set center” and click the map. Apply a radius from 1–450 km (up to 242.98 NM). Click an aircraft or its callsign to inspect it. Distance and bearing are measured from your chosen center.

Positions come from ADSB.lol, with OpenSky as an automatic fallback. The active provider appears above the map. A callsign is the transmitted flight identifier, which may differ from the booking flight number. Registration and aircraft type are shown when supplied. Origin, destination and airline are not guessed. Follow the external research links for more information.

Altitude is barometric feet, not height above terrain. Groundspeed is knots. Vertical rate is feet per minute. Tracks are observed positions collected while this page is open, not full flight history. Dashed tracks have observation gaps over 45 seconds.

Freshness and coverage

ADSB.lol refreshes every 15 seconds while this tab is visible. If it fails, the app automatically tries anonymous OpenSky at 60-second intervals, then retries the primary provider after five minutes. OpenSky has a 400-credit daily allowance per IP, with larger areas costing more; it does not supply registration or aircraft type. Older positions are faded after 30 seconds and removed after 90 seconds. Empty results mean no recent positions were reported, not that the sky is empty. Pausing or switching tabs stops polling. Errors trigger increasing retry delays; the app never substitutes sample aircraft.

Publish on GitHub Pages

  1. Upload this file as index.html to the root of your repository.
  2. In repository Settings → Pages, choose “Deploy from a branch”, your branch, and the root folder.
  3. Open the HTTPS Pages URL once deployment finishes.

Everything runs in this one file. There is no build, server, library CDN, secret, login, or proxy. Internet is needed for flight data, place searches and map tiles. GitHub Pages cannot hide API secrets or fix a provider’s CORS policy. If the public feed becomes unavailable or requires authentication, this zero-backend version will report the failure.

Service use & privacy

For personal, non-commercial use. Open-Meteo’s free geocoding API is non-commercial. ADSB.lol uses dynamic rate limits and may change access requirements. OpenSky anonymous access is limited to 400 credits per day per IP. Both providers must permit browser cross-origin requests; the app cannot guarantee their availability. OpenStreetMap tiles are best effort: normal interactive viewing only, with browser caching and attribution. Larger deployments should arrange appropriate provider capacity.

Your center is sent to the active flight provider (ADSB.lol or OpenSky); a typed city query is sent to Open-Meteo. Map tile requests reveal the viewed area to OpenStreetMap. The last area is stored only in this browser. Share area includes coordinates and radius in the URL. No analytics are installed.
